Not just how much we supervise, but what our supervision teaches.

The conventional choice

Annotate in detail.

Dense labels provide explicit spatial targets, but annotation becomes costly as data scales.

The practical alternative

Learn with less.

Weak supervision learns localization from coarse labels, reducing the need for dense annotation.

The new question

What if labels are automated?

MLLMs and VLMs make dense labels easier to obtain. But are more detailed labels always better?

The central question

Q. If dense supervision is easy to obtain,
is full supervision always the best choice?

A. Not necessarily. More detailed labels can still teach the wrong thing.

More supervision is not
always better supervision.

In diffusion-based image editing, artifacts can extend beyond the intended edit area. Pixel masks can then label transferable traces as authentic, providing misleading supervision and hurting generalization.

Weak supervision offers a natural way to ease this mismatch. Image-level real/fake labels do not force every out-of-mask region to be treated as authentic. Our method, ReGFLoW, builds on this principle with reconstruction-guided, artifact-centric learning.

Sometimes, less supervision can lead to better generalization.

A new weakly supervised formulation.

ReGFLoW is the first weakly supervised framework for diffusion-edited fake region localization in generic visual content, to the best of our knowledge. Prior formulations for conventional manipulation or face-centric edits do not readily transfer to spatially diffuse, non-semantic artifacts.

Meet ReGFLoW.

Reconstruction-Guided Fake Localization under Weak supervision: use diffusion reconstruction cues to learn localization from image-level real/fake labels.

1

Reconstruction prior

A frozen diffusion VAE provides reconstruction error as spatial guidance.

2

Feature and score guidance

Gated fusion and a logit bias inject the prior into both feature and score spaces.

3

Artifact-centric MIL

Aggregate suspicious patches and rank fake-image evidence above real-image evidence.

Takeaway

Before asking for more supervision,
ask whether it is the right supervision.

The right choice depends on the task, the data, and what the labels represent. Diffusion-edited localization shows that weak supervision can be more than a cheaper alternative: it can be a better route to generalization.
